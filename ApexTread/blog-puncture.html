Repair or replace? Reading a puncture correctly

Tyre Care · 6 min read · ApexTread journal · Workshop journal

A small nail in the tread can look simple from the outside, but the correct decision depends on where the damage is, how large it is and what happened to the casing while pressure was low. The tyre needs to be assessed as a complete structure, not just as a visible hole.

Location matters

Repairable damage is normally limited to an appropriate area of the main tread. Damage close to the shoulder or in the sidewall flexes differently and is not treated like a straightforward central tread puncture.

The angle and size of the injury also matter. A workshop should follow the repair limits appropriate to the tyre and repair system rather than treating every nail hole the same way.

Why the tyre should be removed

Removing the tyre allows the inner liner, casing and the area around the injury to be inspected properly. A vehicle driven for too long at very low pressure can create heat and structural damage that is not visible from outside.

An external plug may stop air escaping, but it cannot show whether the inside of the tyre has been weakened.

Repair history and remaining condition

Remaining tread depth, tyre age, existing sidewall damage and previous repairs all affect the decision. A tyre with multiple injuries or a damaged casing may not be a sensible candidate even if the latest puncture looks small.

A proper repair should restore the seal and treat the injury path according to the repair method, rather than simply filling the visible opening.

What to do after discovering a puncture

If pressure is dropping quickly, avoid continuing at normal speed. Use the vehicle’s safe recovery method, spare or assistance option as appropriate. If the loss is slow, keep the tyre correctly inflated only long enough to reach a professional inspection safely.

After repair, monitor pressure over the next few days. A recurring loss means the tyre, valve, rim or repair area needs another check.

Quick takeaway

The visible nail is only the starting point. A safe repair decision comes from the puncture location, internal casing condition, repair history and remaining tyre condition.

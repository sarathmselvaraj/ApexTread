Why a straight steering wheel saves tyres

Alignment · 6 min read · ApexTread journal · Workshop journal

A steering wheel that sits off-centre is more than an annoyance. It can be a clue that the wheels are not pointing where the vehicle manufacturer intended. Even a small geometry error can make a healthy tyre scrub across the road surface instead of rolling cleanly.

What wheel alignment is actually measuring

Modern four-wheel alignment compares toe, camber and other geometry values with the vehicle manufacturer’s targets. Toe describes whether the wheels point slightly inward or outward when viewed from above. When toe is outside tolerance, the tread can be dragged sideways on every journey.

The result is often feathered or one-sided wear, reduced steering confidence and a steering wheel that no longer sits straight when the vehicle travels on a level road.

Signs that make an alignment check worthwhile

Book a geometry check after a strong pothole or kerb impact, suspension or steering work, or when fitting new tyres if the old set shows uneven wear. Steering pull, an off-centre wheel and rapid wear on one shoulder are also useful warning signs.

Do not wait for a new tyre to develop the same pattern. Measuring first helps separate a geometry issue from pressure, tyre construction or suspension wear.

What a professional workshop should do

The vehicle should be inspected for obvious tyre or suspension problems before adjustment. Alignment heads or targets are then fitted to the wheels and the current measurements are compared with the correct vehicle data.

Where adjustment is available, the technician brings the geometry back into tolerance and recentres the steering wheel. A before-and-after printout makes the work transparent and gives you a useful record for the next inspection.

How to protect the new setting

Keep tyre pressures correct, avoid heavy impacts where possible and pay attention to fresh steering changes. If the car receives suspension work or suffers a significant pothole strike, geometry can change again even if the tyres still look healthy.

Checking early is usually cheaper than replacing a tyre that has been worn unevenly while the vehicle still had plenty of tread elsewhere.

Quick takeaway

If the steering wheel is no longer centred or the tread is wearing differently from side to side, measure the geometry before assuming the tyre itself is the problem.

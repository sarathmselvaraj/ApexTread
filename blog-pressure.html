The 5-minute pressure habit before a motorway run

Tyre Care · 5 min read · ApexTread journal · Workshop journal

Tyre pressure changes the shape of the contact patch, the amount of heat the tyre creates and the way the sidewall works. A five-minute check before a motorway journey can catch a slow leak and prevent an under-inflated tyre from spending hours at sustained speed.

Check pressures when the tyres are cold

Measure before the car has travelled far or after it has been parked long enough to cool. Driving creates heat and temporarily raises pressure, which can hide an under-inflated starting point.

Use a reliable gauge and compare all four tyres. A repeated difference at one corner deserves attention because it may point to a valve leak, puncture or rim-sealing issue.

Use the vehicle placard, not the sidewall maximum

The recommended pressure is normally listed on the vehicle placard, handbook or fuel-flap area. It may provide different values for normal use and a heavily loaded vehicle.

The large pressure figure moulded into a tyre sidewall is not the everyday recommendation for your specific car. Following the vehicle data keeps the tyre working in the range intended by the manufacturer.

Combine pressure with a visual inspection

While you are at each wheel, scan the tread and sidewall. Look for nails, screws, cuts, bulges, fresh impact marks and uneven wear. Check the inner and outer shoulders rather than judging the tyre from the centre only.

A pressure reading can be normal immediately after topping up, while visible damage still makes the tyre unsuitable for a long high-speed journey.

What to do when TPMS is warning

Treat a pressure warning as a reason to measure, not automatically as a failed sensor. Correct the actual pressures first and inspect for a leak. Some vehicles need a reset or a short drive before the system recognises the corrected values.

If the warning returns despite correct pressure, a workshop can check sensor status, valve condition and the tyre itself before replacing electronic parts.

Quick takeaway

Cold pressures, a quick tread scan and a look at each sidewall should be part of every long-journey check — especially when the vehicle is carrying extra passengers or luggage.
